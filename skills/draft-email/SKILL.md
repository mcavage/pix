# draft-email

One email, one ask. Before typing a word, answer three questions:
- Who reads this, and what do they care about?
- What is the single thing they must do (or know) after reading?
- What is the deadline or next step?

If you cannot answer all three, ask the user before drafting.

## Structure

**Subject:** specific and action-oriented. Include the topic and the ask or
context in brackets when helpful: `[Project Alpha] Approval needed by Fri`.
Vague subjects get ignored.

**Opening sentence:** state the point. Not "I hope this finds you well" or
"Just wanted to follow up". The first sentence is the email's headline.

**Body:** one short paragraph per idea. Lead with what the reader needs to
act on, not with background context. Background goes last, or in an attached
doc.

**Close:** one specific ask, not a vague "let me know". Name the action and
the date: "Can you approve by Thursday EOD?" If no response is needed, say so.

## Length

- Routine (request, update, FYI): 150 words max.
- Complex (proposal, escalation, multi-topic): 300 words max.
- If it needs more than 300 words, the email is probably a doc. Link the doc
  and use the email as a short cover note.

## Tone

Match the relationship. Formal for first contact or sensitive topics; direct
and collegial for ongoing relationships. Never hedge with qualifiers ("just",
"a bit", "I was wondering if maybe"). State the ask plainly.

## Anti-patterns to cut

Remove: "Per our conversation", "As per my last email", "Circle back",
"Touch base", "Moving forward", "Hope this finds you well."
Replace passive voice with active. Replace "we would like to" with "we want to."

After drafting, read the email as the recipient. If the ask is not obvious in
the first three sentences, revise.
